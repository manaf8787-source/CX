## 2. The template every standard follows

Each document is written in Arabic and English, one document per standard, so the portal can load it:

1. **The rule in one sentence**, one a shopper can check and a customer would recognise.
2. **The measure:** what is observed, the threshold and the scale. A standard without a measure is a value statement, and the platform cannot use it.
3. **The instruments that read it:** shopper, channel signal, survey, journey, L&D.
4. **An example of done right and an example of done wrong.**
5. **Environment variants**, if any: what differs and why.
6. **Exceptions.**
7. **The owner who fixes a breach.**
8. **The training material it points to.**
9. **Effective date, version, approver and next review date.** Gap 12 shows almost no document has these today.

**Two rules are scored from day one, whatever the documents say**, because they hurt the customer directly:

- No information or promise outside the approved sheets.
- No contact outside official channels.

Everything else is observed only in the first shopper wave and scored from the wave after its standard is approved.

The SOPs describe who does what, but almost never what gets written down, where, by which clock, and under which code. The portal can only count what a system records. Today most of the facts it needs are typed by hand into a sheet, held in a second system, or never recorded at all.
